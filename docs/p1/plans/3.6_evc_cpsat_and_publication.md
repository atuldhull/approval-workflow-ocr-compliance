## Implementation

1. `scheduler_cpsat.py` is pure. `solve_cpsat(problem)` takes the 3.5 `SchedulingProblem` and returns a `SchedulingPlan("CPSAT", ...)` plus numeric solver facts; `plan_cpsat` returns the plan. Variables: one boolean per (request, slot) where `select_panel(selector_at(problem, request, ()), ...)` fills a panel against existing reservations, with the request's earliest date, allow_late/planning_until and the slot's residual capacity respected, limited to the request's earliest 12 feasible slots plus EDF's own slot (`max_candidate_slots`, so wide late horizons stay solvable and the hint stays reachable); per-expert booleans over that slot's qualified experts are a feasibility witness only. Constraints: exact panel size, at most one slot per request, at most one booking per expert per date/part across all slots at that time, slot capacity in panels minus existing allocations.
2. Objective, lexicographic through computed weights, follows `metrics.comparison_key`: maximise scheduled requests, then minimise total lateness, maximum lateness (an explicit bound variable) and total waiting. The EDF plan is the solution hint.
3. Panels: the selector forms real panels through `selector_at` in slot order (most urgent request first within a slot). An unfillable choice is forbidden and the model re-solved, up to 5 rounds. The result is scored with `evaluate_schedule`; if EDF scores better on the comparison key, EDF's allocations are kept and `fallback_to_edf` is reported. A nonzero violation count raises SCHEDULER_PLAN_INVALID and nothing is persisted.
4. Determinism: one worker, seed 7, `max_deterministic_time` 2.0 as the effective limit and a 60 s wall-clock cap only as a safety stop. Runtime is measured only by the benchmark script.
5. Propose: `scheduler_edf.handle` stays the single command. `ScheduleCommand` gains `algorithm` ("EDF" default, or "CPSAT") and optional `slot_capacity`. After the scheduling advisory lock, `slot_capacity` creates a canonical slot row (UUID5 per date/part, that capacity) only for horizon date/parts without any slot; existing rows keep their capacity. The locked problem goes to `plan_edf` or `solve_cpsat`; persistence, events and request states are the 3.5 ones with `algorithm` taken from the plan, and solver facts are added to the numeric run parameters (`cpsat_*`). The RO own-region check now runs before the platform policy check, which receives the officer's own region spelling: the merged platform policy compares RO regions exactly.
6. Publish (`schedule_service.publish`, one unit of work): authorize `evc.schedule.publish` (bureau only) on kind `evc`; lock the run, its slot rows and requests. The run must be PROPOSED with drafts; each request must still be PROPOSED and its case is rechecked through CasePort/AuthzPort. Rebuild a `SchedulingProblem` for the run's horizon from current facts with published commitments of other runs as the only reservations and existing allocations; this run's drafts must not block themselves. Double booking → 409 EVC_EXPERT_DOUBLE_BOOKED, slot capacity → 409 EVC_SLOT_CAPACITY_EXHAUSTED, any other `evaluate_schedule` violation → 409 EVC_PUBLISH_RECHECK_FAILED. All drafts are published in one UPDATE; the published-only unique index remains the final guard and its violation maps to EVC_EXPERT_DOUBLE_BOOKED. Requests and run become PUBLISHED; one evc.assignment_published.v1 per assignment is appended to the application aggregate. Any failure rolls back everything.
7. APIs: `POST /api/evc/schedule:propose` {run_id, algorithm, horizon_start, horizon_end (under 120 days), region?, slot_capacity?} → 201 and `POST /api/evc/schedule:publish` {run_id} → 200, both through `idempotent_response` with an optional Idempotency-Key. Responses carry the selector label, SIMULATED/DEMO_ASSUMPTION status and assumption code EDF_PLANNING_UNCONFIRMED; unscheduled requests carry the 3.5 reason NO_FEASIBLE_SLOT.
8. Lifecycle: QUEUED → PROPOSED → PUBLISHED → DONE. Workflow cancellation of a QUEUED or PROPOSED request withdraws its unpublished drafts; a published assignment still fails closed with EVC_SCHEDULE_CANCELLATION_REQUIRED until an unpublication lifecycle exists. `EvcPort.status` returns PUBLISHED once published (no port change), for a later complete_evc guard owned by workflow.

## Reconciliation with the earlier parallel implementation

An earlier P1 branch implemented EDF and CP-SAT independently of 3.5. Where they overlapped, 3.5 is kept as written. From that branch only CP-SAT, publication, slot creation with a DEMO capacity, idempotent HTTP commands and the benchmark were kept, adapted to the 3.5 types. Removed: its own EDF, its Calendar/ScheduleItem types and metrics, the one-visit-per-expert-per-day cap, the max_lateness_days parameter (the horizon plus allow_late bound lateness now), per-request explanation codes, draft supersession on re-proposal (3.5 proposals are reservations and are not re-planned) and its own schedule_proposed payload.

## Benchmark — `python generator/cases/evc_benchmark.py`

Seeded DEMO pool from seed_evc_demo.demo_rows (30 experts, 9 DEMO regions, 60 days, 3 COIs); 361 synthetic requests (seed 26262, 0-2 arrivals per region per day for 40 days, 35% from the COI institution where one exists), 7-day due window, panel size 3, one AM and one PM slot per day with capacity 4 panels. One immutable SchedulingProblem per scenario feeds both planners and `compare_plans` scores them (same fingerprint, recomputed metrics). Late planning off: horizon ends at the last due date (47 days). Late planning on (allow_late): horizon runs 7 days further (54 days). Pure, no database; runtime measured on the development laptop, single worker.

| scenario | algorithm | scheduled / 361 | late | total lateness | max lateness | total waiting (days) | violations | runtime |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| late planning off | EDF | 360 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 982 | 0 | 12.5 s |
| late planning off | CP-SAT | **361** | 0 | 0 | 0 | 946 | 0 | 32.1 s |
| late planning on | EDF | 361 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 992 | 0 | 14.9 s |
| late planning on | CP-SAT | 361 | **0** | **0** | **0** | 946 | 0 | 38.0 s |

CP-SAT status OPTIMAL for the candidate-limited model in both scenarios, no repair rounds, no EDF fallback. Before the 12-slot candidate limit the late-planning model exhausted its deterministic limit without a solution and fell back to EDF, which is why the limit exists. Without the old one-visit-per-expert-per-day cap the DEMO pool is not overloaded: EDF already places nearly every request, and CP-SAT's gain is the one request EDF drops (or places late) plus about 4% less waiting. The pure tests add a hand-built case where EDF schedules 1 of 2 and CP-SAT 2 of 2, and a seeded 3-region, 8-day slice.

## Source and integration limits

The SIMULATED selector stand-in is still not the official authorised selector and P3 identity lookup is still absent. No unpublication, discarding of a proposed run, rescheduling of published visits, expert notification or visit-outcome recording exists. Slot capacity, AM/PM parts and late planning are DEMO ASSUMPTIONs. Large national runs take tens of seconds; regional runs are the intended use.
